# Presentation Script — 5 Minutes Total
# Critical Corporate Document Management
# Speaker: Wenjun Teng (负责介绍 Project 部分)

================================================================================
SECTION 1: Introduce the Project (2 minutes)
================================================================================

## Slide 1 — Title (15 seconds)
"Hi everyone, I'm Wenjun from the Ivey MSc AFP team. Today I'm going to walk you through our project with Southlake Health — Critical Corporate Document Management."

"Our team has five members: Celia, Nicole, Susu, Evie, and myself. We're here to help Southlake address a real and pressing problem: scattered legal documents and the compliance risks that come with them."

---

## Slide 2 — The Problem (35 seconds)
"Here's what we heard in our kickoff meeting. Southlake's critical corporate documents — contracts, MOUs, NDAs, policies — are scattered across network drives, OneDrive, third-party vendors, and frankly, some still exist only as paper copies on people's desks."

"But the real concern isn't just organization. It's enforceability. Past contracts have been signed by individuals without proper signing authority, which means those agreements may not be legally binding on the organization."

"Your internal policy requires two authorized Vice Presidents to sign any document that binds the corporation. But right now, there's no systematic way to verify that compliance across your entire contract portfolio."

---

## Slide 3 — Our Approach (30 seconds)
"So our approach is structured in three phases over 12 weeks."

"Phase 1 is mapping and classification — we build a standardized document taxonomy so you know exactly what you have and where it lives. This is already underway."

"Phase 2 is compliance and risk assessment — we define the rules for signing authority verification and classify risks across four dimensions: operational, financial, reputational, and continuity."

"Phase 3 is building and recommending — we prototype an AI-assisted review system and deliver a full cost and staffing analysis for operational implementation."

---

## Slide 7 — Progress & Next Steps (15 seconds)
"On progress: we've completed the kickoff and the SOW is signed. We're currently waiting on two things from your side — the de-identified contract templates for our AI prototype work, and Legal Counsel Tonya's return to confirm privilege protection for our project materials. Once those come through, we move into Phase 2 risk classification immediately."

================================================================================
SECTION 2: The 8 Deliverables (2 minutes)
================================================================================

## Slide 4 — Deliverables Overview (20 seconds)
"Now let me walk you through our eight deliverables. There are four core deliverables, two stretch deliverables, and two administrative requirements."

---

## Slide 5 — Core Deliverables 1–4 (50 seconds)
"Deliverable 1 is the Document Taxonomy and Classification Framework. This is the foundation — we define what types of documents exist across the organization, create a standardized metadata schema, and design it for integration with your existing SharePoint or OneDrive setup."

"Deliverable 2 is the Risk Exposure Assessment. This is where we build the framework to identify documents that are missing, expired, not executed according to policy, or held by individuals instead of the organization."

"Deliverable 3 is Risk Category Exploration. We work with your subject matter experts to classify risk across four categories: operational risk, financial risk, reputational risk, and continuity or succession risk. Each category has specific indicators we'll track."

"Deliverable 4 is the Risk Reduction Recommendations. This isn't just a list of problems — we give you a prioritized action plan with full cost estimates, FTE staffing requirements, and operational overhead analysis. This is what makes the project actionable."

---

## Slide 6 — Stretch & Administrative Deliverables 5–8 (50 seconds)
"Deliverable 5 is the AI Review Prototype — this is the stretch deliverable that really excites us. It's an AI-assisted 'tickler' system that uses de-identified templates to automatically flag missing fields, expiry dates, renewal triggers, obligation and milestone deadlines, variable financial terms, retention violations, duplicates, and conflicting versions."

"Deliverable 6 is the Governance Policy Recommendation. We don't just fix the current mess — we recommend an intake process and governance policy so future documents follow the same standards from day one, preventing fragmentation before it happens."

"Deliverable 7 covers our bi-weekly status reports. Every two weeks you'll receive a written report with project status, completed and planned activities, an earned value graphic, and a risks and issues log. You'll always know where we stand."

"And Deliverable 8 is the final synchronous presentation — a 30 to 45 minute formal presentation to all stakeholders, followed by Q&A and the client acceptance report."

"Important note on all of this: every piece of AI development complies with Southlake's AI governance framework. Microsoft ecosystem, Canadian-hosted models only, no US cloud infrastructure, and full documentation so your team can replicate everything we build."

================================================================================
CLOSING (15 seconds)
================================================================================
"What excites me about this project is that it's not theoretical. The risk is real, your stakeholders are engaged, and the output will actually be used. Thank you, and we look forward to working with you."

## Tips
- Speak slowly and clearly; 2 minutes per section is tight
- Make eye contact with stakeholders, not just the screen
- The yellow script notes in each slide are memory anchors — don't read verbatim
- If running short on time, compress Slide 7 (progress) to one sentence
- If running long, skip the detailed descriptions on Slides 5-6 and summarize in one line each
